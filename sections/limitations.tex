\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Calibration and labels.} The controller needs an operator label and a per-domain calibration of the frontier and the learning curve, and it does not train without them. \Cref{sec:robust} bounds the cost of a stale or missing label on fixed mixtures; drifting mixtures are not covered.

\paragraph{Prediction error and engine overhead.} The controller acts on predicted throughput and TPOT, not on measured latency (\cref{app:controller}); at break-even, as on the code--chat mixture, a frontier error of a few percent decides whether training pays. The switch can gain at most what the engine's speculation-off state costs (\nv{zh.switch_vs_plain.abs}--\nv{code.switch_vs_plain.abs}\% in vLLM, \nv{sgl.off_vs_plain.c256}\% in SGLang; \cref{sec:sglang}).

\paragraph{Scope.} We evaluate two engines (vLLM 0.27.1 and, with five policies, SGLang 0.5.10), one GPU type (H100), two targets of one model family, EAGLE-3 drafters with $k{=}3$, greedy decoding with natural stopping at up to 512 tokens (\cref{tab:protocol}), and single-GPU serving without prefix caching; \pol{TIDE} reimplements only TIDE's training triggers on a shared GPU; the live workload is a synthetic chat--new-domain--chat sequence, with other traffic shapes covered only by two fixed mixtures and in simulation.

\paragraph{Statistics and development.} Each cell has three paired repetitions on \nv{hosts} nodes; node interference slowed \nv{scan.flagged.word} baseline runs, replaced by reruns (\cref{app:rerun}), and paired differences of a few percent between adaptive policies are within the run-to-run spread. The rules were developed on runs from the same prompt pools in different orders, so the frozen evaluation does not test them on unseen prompts.
