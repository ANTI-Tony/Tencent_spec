
\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

Improving drafter acceptance does not always improve serving throughput, and the value of adaptation differs across workloads. On Qwen3-8B Chinese, disabling speculation is more effective than further training; on the MoE target, speculation remains profitable and adaptation provides the main gain. Qwen3-8B code falls between these cases: training improves the drafter, but excessive training can consume more serving capacity than the improvement recovers. The ablations confirm that the payback rule limits this interference under saturation, while the explicit stopping tests contribute little beyond the payback decision in that setting. The code--chat mixture exposes a harder case. Although the calibrated learning ceiling exceeds the break-even acceptance, the cost of reaching it is not recovered over the observed workload. Training decisions near this boundary are therefore sensitive to calibration errors and the remaining duration of a workload. The controller also requires operator-provided domain labels and per-domain calibration, and does not initiate training without the required calibration. The fixed-mixture experiments cover stale and missing labels, but not continuously drifting workload compositions. Even with an accurate decision, the realized benefit depends on the serving engine: disabling speculation does not fully recover plain-decoding performance when the drafter remains loaded, as observed in both vLLM and SGLang (\cref{sec:sglang}). The controller also relies on predicted throughput and TPOT rather than directly enforcing measured latency (\cref{app:controller}). Our live evaluation is limited to two serving engines, H100 GPUs, two Qwen3 targets, and EAGLE-3 drafters with $k{=}3$, using greedy decoding without prefix caching. Workloads follow a synthetic chat--new-domain--chat sequence or one of two fixed mixtures; other traffic patterns are examined only in simulation. The TIDE-style baseline reproduces its training triggers on a shared GPU rather than its original deployment. Each configuration has three paired repetitions across \nv{hosts} nodes, with interference-affected runs replaced under the documented protocol (\cref{app:rerun}). Small differences between adaptive policies remain within run-to-run variability, and reordering prompts from the development pools does not establish generalization to unseen prompts.
